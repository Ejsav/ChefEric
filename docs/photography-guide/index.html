The placeholders are not empty boxes on purpose: each one is a sketch of the light and color the real photograph should have. Use them as a guide to mood. The photograph replaces them exactly, at the same size and shape.

For every photograph

  • Real light only. Candles, windows, lamps, sunset. No flash, no bright overhead light.
  • Warm and dark. Deep brown shadows, amber candlelight, ivory linen, aged brass. Like a still from a film.
  • Shallow focus. Fast lens, f/1.4–2.8. Background lights melt into soft circles.
  • No guest faces. Hands, shoulders, backs and silhouettes only. Discretion is part of the brand.
  • Chef Ray is always real. Any photo of him, his family or his past must be a real photograph. Never a stand-in or an imitation.
  • Nothing branded. No logos, wine labels, boat names or recognizable homes.
  • Leave room at the edges. The website trims about 5% from each edge and adds a caption along the bottom.
  • One look throughout. Edit all photos with the same warm, slightly muted grade.

Delivery

Format
JPEG, sRGB, highest quality. Keep the RAW files.

At least 2400 × 3000 px. Photos 09–19.

01

EssentialCaption on site: “Your dining room, after dark”

A home in Connecticut

setting-residence.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-residence.jpg

The first room a visitor sees in “Compose an evening”. It is the default view, so it shows up on the home page before anyone clicks.

What to capture

A real Connecticut dining room at night, set for a private dinner, photographed from the doorway at standing height. It should feel like a home, not a hotel.

In the frame

  • Polished wood table for about eight, dressed in ivory linen.
  • Lit candles in aged-brass holders down the middle; wine already poured.
  • Classic New England room: painted wood paneling, a dark window, a brass chandelier dimmed low.
  • Through an open door on the right: a slice of warm kitchen light with a figure working at the stove, out of focus.
  • Lived-in details in shadow: books on a sideboard, a framed painting.

Light & color

Candles and the dimmed chandelier only. Warm amber on the table, deep brown shadow in the corners.

Avoid

Bright overhead light, flash, guests' faces, anything that identifies the homeowner.

What's needed

A real dining room in Connecticut; table linen, candles, glassware. Chef Ray optional (as the blurred figure in the kitchen).

02

ImportantCaption on site: “Above the avenue”

A Manhattan penthouse

setting-penthouse.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-penthouse.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “A Manhattan penthouse”.

What to capture

A dinner table for six in front of floor-to-ceiling windows in a high-floor New York apartment at night, with the city glowing out of focus beyond the glass.

In the frame

  • Table in the lower half of the frame: ivory linen, low candles, crystal glasses.
  • City lights through the window fill the upper half: thousands of soft points of light.
  • Minimal, expensive interior: dark wood floor, one sculptural lamp, an artwork in shadow.

Light & color

Candlelight on the table, cool city glow on the glass. Warm inside, cool outside.

Avoid

Recognizable landmarks or skyline, reflections of the photographer in the glass, guests' faces.

What's needed

A high-floor apartment with big windows (a rental or client's home with permission).

Completes the setCaption on site: “Long table, late August”

A Hamptons estate

setting-estate.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-estate.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “A Hamptons estate”.

What to capture

A long outdoor table for twenty on the lawn of a shingled summer estate, about 20 minutes before sunset, shot from one end so the table runs diagonally into the frame.

In the frame

  • Natural linen, low garden flowers and herbs, glass hurricane lanterns.
  • Tall privet hedges and a shingled house with white trim softly in the background.
  • Optional: two distant staff silhouettes carrying platters.

Light & color

Low golden sun from behind the table so glasses glow and edges catch gold. Sky warm gold fading to peach.

Avoid

Harsh midday sun, recognizable houses, guests' faces.

What's needed

A large lawn or garden; a long table setup. Golden hour only.

Completes the setCaption on site: “Doors open to the bay”

A waterfront home in Florida

setting-waterfront.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-waterfront.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “A waterfront home in Florida”.

What to capture

Looking out from inside a waterfront home at blue hour: tall glass doors folded open, a table set on the terrace, calm water beyond.

In the frame

  • Pale stone floor, white curtains moving slightly.
  • Terrace table for eight: white linen, low candles, bowls of citrus, a whole roasted fish on a platter.
  • Calm bay in deep indigo, palm fronds in silhouette at the edges, a few warm dock lights.

Light & color

Blue hour (just after sunset): indigo sky and water, warm candles on the table.

Avoid

Bright daytime sun, visible neighbors' houses, guests' faces.

What's needed

A waterfront home (Florida or any warm coast).

ImportantCaption on site: “Anchored, second course”

Aboard a yacht

setting-yacht.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-yacht.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “Aboard a yacht”.

What to capture

The back deck of a large yacht at anchor just after sunset, table set for ten, open water filling the background. Shot at seated height so the table edge is the foreground.

In the frame

  • Teak table, ivory linen, candles in glass (they stay lit in the breeze), crystal stemware.
  • Polished rail and a coiled rope for a sense of place.
  • Horizon of fading orange and deep blue; a far-off coastline with a few lights.
  • Optional: a crew member's white-sleeved hand placing a plate.

Light & color

Last light of sunset plus candles.

Avoid

Boat name, flags, registration numbers, guests' faces.

What's needed

A yacht (charter or client, with permission). Calm evening.

ImportantCaption on site: “Lanterns along the dock”

On a private island

setting-island.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-island.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “On a private island”. This is one of Chef Ray's signature stories.

What to capture

From the end of a long wooden dock at dusk, looking back toward the shore: lanterns line both sides of the dock and lead the eye to a single lit table under a simple canopy.

In the frame

  • Weathered silver-grey planks, glowing lanterns on both sides.
  • At the far end: a table for six under an open canopy among tropical trees.
  • Clear dark water reflecting the lantern light in long streaks.
  • No other buildings or boats in sight.

Light & color

Dusk: violet sky with a thin orange horizon, warm lanterns.

Avoid

Resort signage, other guests, anything that identifies the island or client.

What's needed

Ideally a real photo from one of Chef Ray's island engagements (client details removed). Otherwise a private dock at dusk.

Somewhere abroad

setting-abroad.jpgpublic/images/possibilities/setting-abroad.jpg

Shown when a visitor picks “Somewhere abroad”. It connects quietly to his Italian roots.

What to capture

A stone terrace of a villa in Italy or on a Mediterranean lake at the end of a summer day, table set for eight, hills or water in soft haze beyond.

In the frame

  • Weathered stone balustrade, pots of lemon trees, vines on a pergola overhead.
  • Rustic white linen, carafes of wine, bowls of figs and lemons, low candles.
  • Distant hills or lake in blue-gold haze.

Light & color

Warm late sun from the side, long shadows across stone.

Avoid

Famous landmarks, hotel branding, guests' faces.

What's needed

Can come from a future trip; a European terrace at sunset.

09

EssentialCaption on site: “The Chef”

Chef Ray in his kitchen

chef-portrait.jpgpublic/images/home/chef-portrait.jpg

The most important photograph on the website. It introduces Chef Ray on the home page next to “Raised on Sunday gravy. Made on the line.”

What to capture

A real, natural portrait of Chef Ray standing in a kitchen, framed from mid-thigh up, one hand resting on a worn wooden counter. He looks just past the lens, as if listening to someone: confident, warm, unposed.

In the frame

  • Wardrobe: charcoal or black chef's jacket with sleeves pushed up, or a dark tailored shirt with a long dark apron. A towel tucked at the hip.
  • Background softly out of focus: hanging copper pots, a shelf of glass jars, the blue-orange glow of a burner.
  • His body turned slightly to camera right, face toward the window light.

Light & color

One soft window light from camera left lighting one side of his face. The right side of the frame falls into warm shadow.

Avoid

Studio backdrop, flash, big smile at camera, arms crossed, heavy retouching.

What's needed

Chef Ray. A kitchen with a window and some character (his own kitchen is ideal).

I. The Private Table

private-table.jpgpublic/images/experiences/private-table.jpg

Beside “The Private Table”, his core private-dinner service.

What to capture

A candlelit dinner table in a private home, photographed from a seated guest's eye level looking across the table. The minute before guests sit down.

In the frame

  • Foreground: table edge in ivory linen, white plate with a fine brass rim, folded napkin with a printed menu card tucked in, heavy silver cutlery.
  • Middle: three lit candles in aged-brass holders, crystal glasses with red wine.
  • Background: two empty upholstered chairs across the table, waiting, in warm darkness.

Light & color

Candles only.

Avoid

People, bright room lights, logos on bottles.

What's needed

Same dining room as Photo 01 works well. Menu card prop.

II. The Island & The Water

island-water.jpgpublic/images/experiences/island-water.jpg

Beside “The Island & The Water”: yachts, islands and destinations.

What to capture

A dinner table on a stone terrace above open water at the very end of dusk, shot from behind and slightly above one chair.

In the frame

  • Table for four: ivory linen held by brass weights, candles in glass hurricanes, a platter of grilled whole fish with lemons and herbs.
  • Beyond the balustrade: dark open sea; horizon a thin cold blue-grey band with a trace of orange; one distant light of an anchored boat.
  • Top of the frame: night sky.

Light & color

Candlelight against the cool last light over the water.

Avoid

Other buildings, beach crowds, guests' faces.

What's needed

A waterside terrace (coast, lake or island). Shoot at dusk.

III. The Kitchen Lessons

kitchen-lessons.jpgpublic/images/experiences/kitchen-lessons.jpg

Beside “The Kitchen Lessons”, his private cooking classes.

What to capture

Close on two pairs of hands at a floured wooden counter: Chef Ray's hands guiding a guest's hands as they fold eggs into a well of flour to make pasta dough.

In the frame

  • Chef Ray: rolled sleeves, dark apron just visible. Guest: cream knit sleeve.
  • Around them: wooden rolling pin, bench scraper, a bowl of brown eggs, a mound of finished dough under a cloth.
  • Flour dust floating in a shaft of window light.

Light & color

Soft daylight from a window on the left. Brighter and warmer than the evening photos.

Avoid

Faces, messy counters, plastic or branded tools.

What's needed

Chef Ray plus one person as the 'guest' (hands only). Flour, eggs, a wooden board.

IV. The Occasion

occasion.jpgpublic/images/experiences/occasion.jpg

Beside “The Occasion”: celebrations and hosted events.

What to capture

The moment of a toast at a long celebration table in low warm light. A dozen hands raise glasses above the table; nobody's face is shown.

In the frame

  • Hands, sleeves and shoulders only: dark jackets, a silk dress strap, a bracelet catching light.
  • Slight motion blur on the raised glasses.
  • Table: candles, half-finished plates, loose garden roses in burgundy and ivory.
  • Background: a warm, softly lit room with an arched window.

Light & color

Low, warm, candlelit. A little haze is welcome.

Avoid

Recognizable faces, phones, branded bottles.

What's needed

8–12 people as guests (faces turned away or out of frame). Can be staged with friends.

The Chef

Chef Ray's story is told in six eras called The Archive. Each era needs one photograph. Where real photos from his past exist, they are better than anything new.

Photographs 14–19 · 6 shots

14

Completes the setCaption on site: “Sunday, before noon”

I. The family table

01-family-table.jpgpublic/images/archive/01-family-table.jpg

The first era of his story, New Haven. Real history: this should be a real family photograph if one exists.

What to capture

First choice: a real photo from Chef Ray's childhood or youth in New Haven (a crowded Sunday table, a grandparent at the stove), scanned in high resolution. If none exists, shoot a present-day still life instead, never an imitation of an old photo.

In the frame

  • Still-life option: a heavy enamel pot of red sauce simmering on an old white stove, a long wooden spoon across the top, steam rising.
  • Around it: crusty Italian bread on a cloth, a jar of dried oregano, a handwritten recipe card, a glass of red wine.

Light & color

Soft morning window light. Cream, tomato red, warm wood.

Avoid

Fake 'vintage' filters on new photos.

What's needed

Family photo albums (scan at 1200 dpi) or the still-life props.

ImportantCaption on site: “Service, nine o'clock”

II. The line

02-the-line.jpgpublic/images/archive/02-the-line.jpg

Era II of his story: decades in restaurant kitchens.

What to capture

First choice: a real photo of Chef Ray on a restaurant line. Otherwise: a professional kitchen mid-service, shot from the end of the line at hip height, with flame in the foreground pan.

In the frame

  • Stainless steel, a row of burners with tall flames under steel pans, steam.
  • Order tickets hanging on a rail above.
  • A cook in whites with his back to camera, out of focus.

Light & color

Hot orange flame light mixed with cool kitchen fluorescents.

Avoid

Restaurant names, health-code violations, cooks' faces (unless it's Chef Ray).

What's needed

Access to a working restaurant kitchen during or staged as service.

ImportantCaption on site: “A private room”

III. The private rooms

03-private-rooms.jpgpublic/images/archive/03-private-rooms.jpg

Era III: private households, estates and luxury dining.

What to capture

A low-lit private dining room in a grand home: one round table for eight dressed and lit by candles, a slightly open service door spilling warm kitchen light across the floor.

In the frame

  • Dark green silk or deep red lacquered walls; a large oil painting in a gilded frame, in shadow.
  • Crystal chandelier dimmed low.
  • Service door in the left third of the frame.
  • No people.

Light & color

Candles plus the warm spill from the door.

Avoid

Identifiable artwork or homeowner details.

What's needed

An elegant private dining room (estate, club or historic home).

IV. The islands

04-the-islands.jpgpublic/images/archive/04-the-islands.jpg

Era IV: private-island dining. Real history: use a real photo from his work if one exists.

What to capture

First choice: a real photograph from one of Chef Ray's island engagements, with any client details removed. Otherwise: a wooden dock on an island at dusk, lanterns lit, crates of provisions stacked at the near end.

In the frame

  • Weathered dock leading to shore, lanterns on both sides.
  • Wooden crates of lemons, herbs and bottles in the foreground.
  • Calm blue-black water reflecting the lanterns.

Light & color

Dusk: violet sky, thin orange horizon.

Avoid

Anything identifying the client or island.

What's needed

Chef Ray's own photos from past engagements, or the dock from Photo 06 (different angle).

V. The lessons

05-the-lessons.jpgpublic/images/archive/05-the-lessons.jpg

Era V: teaching. Pairs with Photo 12 but is a different, closer moment.

What to capture

Very close on Chef Ray's flour-dusted hands placing a guest's fingers to show how to pinch and shape orecchiette pasta on a wooden board.

In the frame

  • Only hands and forearms: his with rolled dark sleeves, the guest's in a cream knit sleeve.
  • Rows of finished pasta, a bench scraper, a small bowl of semolina.
  • Flour in the air.

Light & color

Window daylight from the side.

Avoid

Faces; making it look identical to Photo 12 (shoot closer, different shape of pasta).

What's needed

Chef Ray plus one 'guest'. Same session as Photo 12.

VI. Wherever the table is

06-now.jpgpublic/images/archive/06-now.jpg

The final era: Chef Ray today. This closes his story, so it must be a real photo of him.

What to capture

Chef Ray at the head of a fully set, candlelit table in a client's dining room, minutes before guests arrive, making one last adjustment to a wine glass. He looks down at it; his face in three-quarter profile, lit by the candles.

In the frame

  • Dark chef's jacket or dark tailored shirt.
  • Long table glowing in ivory and brass, everything perfect.
  • Room elegant and dim around him.

Light & color

Candlelight on his face and hands; the room falls into shadow.

Avoid

Looking at the camera, guests in frame, flash.

What's needed

Chef Ray. Can be shot in the same dining room as Photos 01 and 10.
